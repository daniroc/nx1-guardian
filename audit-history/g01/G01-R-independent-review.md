## Authority boundaries and maintainable architecture

Protocol validation owns syntax/version/escaping/preview only. Authorization owns verified actor, project/repository/ref/head, exact operation/scope, expiry, current policy/lock epoch, review evidence and resources. Capability enforcement owns each credential/adapter invocation. Infrastructure isolation owns filesystem/network/host/process limits. External human administration owns trust-root enrollment, policy activation, release promotion, incident recovery and budget increases. Each layer needs its own observer/test; none can be replaced with agent prose or consensus.

Recommend a standalone Guardian repository with a small reusable contract package and independent service, outside Nexus's execution/deployment pipeline. Preserve NX1 Core as a pinned dependency/artifact with separately approved provenance; never import Nexus private-env, auth, persistence or write composition into the authority. Agents may prepare proposed source in a development checkout but cannot activate/promote/administer it. External human owners control deployments, policy signer, IAM, gateway credentials, audit retention and kill control. A Guardian permit is a mandatory security gate in addition to the consumer's existing review; it cannot bypass Nexus's human preview, DEL confirmation or revision protections.

Trade-offs: a separate repository creates version/promotion overhead and explicit adapters, but avoids silently sharing Nexus release authority and secret context. A minimal deterministic evaluator with a fixed typed allowlist is easier to audit than executable policies or runtime plugins, but cannot approve semantic safety automatically. Prefer a mandatory narrow gateway over direct provider credentials, at the cost of availability/latency and an additional trusted component. Choose VM boundaries for adversarial code, with higher resource cost than processes/containers. Separate audit/witness/kill administration increases operational work but reduces common-mode failure. Do not introduce a distributed orchestration platform merely for the synthetic local harness; distributed durability is a later verified prerequisite.

External admin requirements: admin-only origin/device/profile, phishing-resistant MFA with externally verified fresh session/issuer/audience/role evidence, dual distinct human approval of exact policy digest/version/epoch/expiry/limits and release artifact, protected signer/KMS custody, explicit key rotation/revocation, and separate audit-reader/release/recovery roles. Agent signatures prove at most proposal origin. Policy downgrades, duplicate-reviewer identities, expired approval or changed intent/epoch deny. A real admin may immediately stop, but unlock, trust-root replacement and recovery require independent external approval, witnessed epoch reconciliation and fresh grants. No administrative override endpoint is reachable by an agent; no chat or tool output can substitute for enrollment or recovery authentication.

## Emergency, fail-closed and data lifecycle

Stop admission/delivery and fence queued grants when kill is durably recorded. Distinguish STOP_DURABLE (record exists), STOP_ENFORCED (gateway/supervisor acknowledgements) and STOP_UNCONFIRMED (persistence failed). With outstanding dispatch leases, either fence/wait or classify them in-flight; do not promise no external commits after the stop instant. Revoke session/provider authority independently, terminate workloads where technically possible and retain EFFECT_UNKNOWN until trusted reconciliation. Committed writes, deployment, billing and disclosure are irreversible by kill alone. Lock persists across restart/restore; missing/rolled-back epoch denies. Recovery requires external approval and cannot reuse queued grants.

Invalid/ambiguous messages, expired credentials, absent current policy/identity/clock, corrupt/overflow budget ledger and missing current audit receipt deny before dispatch. Missing telemetry denies new paid admission and retains uncertain reservations. Dependency restoration does not revive exhausted sessions. Failed revoke/cancel/kill preserves lock and uncertainty, alerts external operator and never reports false completion. A malicious trusted gateway may disregard these rules; independent fencing helps only while that infrastructure remains trusted.

Audit prevention means permission/retention enforcement outside agent/executor authority; cryptographic tamper evidence means ordered canonical records, prior digest, externally held signed checkpoints and separately controlled witness. Hashes cannot alone prevent deletion or prove completeness. Compare independent dispatch, usage and kill records when reconstructing an incident. Emergency evidence needs its own durable path if the normal sink fails. Local append-only APIs and ACLs are not production immutable/WORM storage.

Minimize prompts/tool bodies, secrets and identities in logs; never record credentials/headers or uncontrolled diagnostics. Commit payload/intent digests and operational metadata; explicitly approved encrypted incident content belongs in separate restricted storage. G01's proposed 180-day decision, 365-day admin/emergency and 30-day incident retention still needs external privacy approval; it is not an active requirement. Access reads and retention-boundary deletions need external audit. Credential enrollment, issuer custody, short expiry, fencing, revocation confirmation and recovery rotation are separate steps; missing confirmation remains open. Allowed model disclosure and allowed-channel exfiltration remain residual risks.
